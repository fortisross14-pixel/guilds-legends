# UX redesign: HD-2D

This pass replaces the 16-bit JRPG interface (flat royal-blue windows with white pixel frames, a pixel font on all text, nested boxes, art squeezed into frames). It keeps the HD-2D art pack and the screen structure and replaces the visual language with an HD-2D one: pixel-art characters and worlds presented with modern light, depth, elegant type and restrained menus (references: Octopath Traveler I & II, Triangle Strategy, Dragon Quest III HD-2D, Live A Live, Sea of Stars). It is a visual pass only: no engine, rule or state changes, and every action, route and screen is still reachable. The system is documented in [`DESIGN.md`](../DESIGN.md); product intent in [`PRODUCT.md`](../PRODUCT.md).

## Direction

- **Art first.** Every place has its scene full-screen behind the interface (`SceneBackdrop`, portalled into `<body>`): slow drift, depth-of-field blur at the top and bottom edges, warm bloom, vignette, a contrast scrim and drifting dust. The world map is the overworld itself, full-screen. Battles fill the screen.
- **One panel level.** Overlays are dark translucent panels (ink gradient at 84–90%, 14px backdrop blur) with a thin brass edge, small brass corner flourishes and a soft shadow. Inside a panel, grouping is hairlines and spacing only; the former inset boxes and framed cells became hairline-bounded bands and rows.
- **Type.** Cormorant Garamond for titles, place and character names, ceremonies and big numbers; Cinzel for small-caps labels and navigation; Manrope for text, menus and data with tabular numbers. No pixel font in the interface.
- **Menus.** Elegant vertical lists with hairline dividers. One glowing gem cursor per menu glides to the hovered, focused or selected row (`useMenuKeys` / `syncMenuCursors` in `UI.jsx`); the active row gets a soft horizontal light sweep. Buttons are text with a fine brass frame that glows on hover; primary actions are warm gold. Tabs are Cinzel labels with a glowing gold underline.
- **Colour.** Deep blue-brown inks sampled from the scene shadows; warm brass and gold for edges, prestige, money and primary actions; parchment for dialogue; muted HP/MP/damage, rarity and primal colours.
- **Motion.** Panels fade and rise (260ms), the cursor glides (220ms), HP/MP bars ease down with a lagging damage ghost, numbers count up, dialogue types out (click to finish). Ceremonies get a light burst, a slow title reveal and rising sparks. Ambient dust, backdrop drift, sprite idle breathing and an Epic/Legend portrait glint. `prefers-reduced-motion` and the in-game setting remove ambient motion, make transitions instant and show typed text at once.

## What changed per screen

- **Title screen.** The harbor scene full-screen with drift and dust; the crest and an engraved gold serif logo between brass rules with a staged reveal; an elegant Continue / New Game slot menu with the gem cursor.
- **Status bar and dock.** One slim overlay line instead of three boxed rows: date, time commands, location, gold, the three ranks with glowing arrows, status title, and a **Journal** chip showing the next step. The chip opens a dropdown with the next step (Go / Skip tutorial) and the upcoming events. The dock is a floating panel (icons on tablet, a rounded bottom bar on phone). Action messages are a floating toast.
- **Party bar.** Slim translucent plates (portrait, name, activity, HP), collapsible; on phone, portrait + HP only.
- **World map.** The pixel overworld fills the screen with a slow drift, edge fog, warm light over the heartland and fog banks over locked lands. Towns are lit medallion markers on pools of light with serif name banners; the current town has a pulsing ring and a "You are here" tag; reachable routes are fine glowing dotted lines; bosses and the Blackwood have their own markers. The location panel slides in from the right (a bottom sheet on phone); the almanac panels scroll up over the map.
- **Town and Guild Hall.** The town or building scene full-screen with a large serif place name over the art; buildings and rooms are a refined menu panel on one side (icon, serif name, description, count badge); the town's line sits in an italic note on the other side. Heroes in rooms are status rows with serif names.
- **Notice board, tavern, shop, arena, herald, Chronicle pages.** The same panels over their scenes; tab strips with gold underlines; rankings with serif guild names, the player row lit and glowing movement arrows. The shop keeps its buy/sell list, the detail panel with ▲/▼ stat comparison and the gold line.
- **Hero status.** The hero's portrait large on the left, lit by a rarity-coloured glow, with class, tags, vitals and the stat table under it; attributes, equipment, skills, character, relationships and career on the right.
- **Battle.** The scene fills the screen with depth of field, vignette and dust. Enemies stand on soft ground shadows and breathe; target selection shows a pulsing light ring and a gem above the enemy. The tell is a serif banner at the top with the latest message under it. Party plates sit along the bottom; the active hero's plate lifts and glows, and the command menu floats above that hero. Damage and heal numbers are large serif pops with hit and heal effects. Victory and defeat are a ceremony card with a light burst.
- **Moments and dialogue.** Victory (burst, sparks, counting gold, XP per hero, spoils as chips), level up (glowing portrait, stat gains), rank up and title changes (crown, burst), rankings update, nemesis. Story beats, town events, road events and recruit introductions are parchment dialogue boxes with a dark name plate, the portrait of the hero the event names (otherwise the guild crest), typed text and a bobbing continue gem.

## Removed CSS

Rewritten from scratch: `base/tokens.css`, `base/base.css`, `layout/shell.css`, `layout/responsive.css`, `components/art.css`, `components/battle.css`, `components/moments.css`, `screens/home.css`, `screens/world.css`, `screens/town.css`, `screens/hero-status.css`. Swept in place (old tokens renamed, frames removed, labels and names re-typed): the remaining screen files and `components/mission-games.css`. Gone entirely: the pixel window frame system (`--frame`, `--frame-thin`, `--frame-dim` 9-slice border images and every `border-image` use), the ▶ pixel cursor, the Pixelify Sans and Atkinson Hyperlegible fonts, the integer scene scaling (`--scene-k`), the boxed town scene banner, the solid selected-row blocks, the dashed list dividers, the stacked upcoming/objective rows, and the pixel crest component.

## Verification

`npm run build` passes and ESLint is clean. Screenshots at 390×844 and 1440×900 are in [`docs/screenshots/`](screenshots/) for 21 screens: title, new game, world map, town, Guild Hall, tavern, notice board, hero status, shop, active battle, victory, level up, expedition report, council report, town event card, calendar, herald, chronicle, codex, legacy office and facilities. No horizontal page scroll and no console errors at either size. Two review rounds were run against the reference games; the fixes from the second round (enemy line clear of the command menu, scrims behind battle messages and tab strips over bright art, phone status bar width, phone battle tell) were retaken.

## Known gaps

- Navigation, building, room and status-bar icons are still the 12×12 pixel glyph set, now tinted brass with a glow. A fine-line icon set drawn for this language would sit better next to the serif type.
- Enemy names in battle come from the encounter data and sometimes don't match the sprite (for example "Grey Wolf C" under the hooded caster); that is an engine/content mapping, outside this pass.
- Story beats show the guild crest instead of a speaker portrait, because story cards carry no speaker; town events show the portrait of the hero they name.
- Some building screens repeat their name: the place title over the art and the screen's own heading (for example "Herald" and "The Herald").
- Full-page screenshots show the fixed party bar in the middle of long pages; that is how the capture handles fixed elements, not a layout bug.
